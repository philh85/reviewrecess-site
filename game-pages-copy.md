## Review Recess Answer Armada

**URL:** https://reviewrecess.com/games/answer-armada/  
**Title tag:** Answer Armada: Hidden-Ships Review Game | Review Recess  
**Meta description (157 characters):** A whole-class review game for any subject: every correct answer earns one shot at the teacher's hidden ships. Three difficulty levels and six picture styles.  
**H1:** Answer Armada: a hidden-ships review game where every right answer earns a shot  
**Tag:** Whole class vs teacher

### Intro
Review Recess Answer Armada turns any question set into a team mission. The whole class plays together against the teacher. A correct answer earns exactly one shot at a lettered and numbered grid, and the class has to find and sink every hidden ship before the shots run out.

It works for any subject, from early elementary to high school. Younger classes can hunt for dinosaurs, eggs or animals instead of ships, and older classes get the Classic Fleet.

### At a glance
- Grades: Grades 2–12 (picture styles for K–6 and 7–12)
- Subjects: Any subject
- Group size: The whole class against the teacher
- Time: 15–30 minutes

### How to play Answer Armada

**Set up the game**
1. Open the game and choose a picture style: Classic Fleet (Grades 7–12) or one of five K–6 styles: Sea Safari, Dino Dig, Space Explorer, Spring Egg Hunt or Jungle Safari.
2. Pick a difficulty. Easy is a 7 × 5 grid with 5 ships and 28 shots, Normal is 7 × 5 with 3 ships and 21 shots, and Hard is a bigger 9 × 7 grid with 3 ships and 28 shots.
3. Choose a question bank (or use the general review sample), and use ☑ Choose… to untick any questions you don't want today. You can also hide the questions and ask your own out loud.
4. Press Start game and go full screen. The ships are placed at random every game.

**Play a round**
5. Press Next question. The question appears as the largest text on the screen, and the grid stays locked (🔒) until you mark the answer.
6. Call on a student (or use 🎤 Pick a student) to answer. Press Show answer if you want the class to see it.
7. Press ✓ Correct to unlock the grid for exactly one shot. Press ✗ Incorrect to show the answer and move on with no shot.
8. The class calls a square, such as C4, and you click it. The game shows a miss, a hit or a sunk ship. Extra clicks are ignored, and the next question comes up by itself with the grid locked again.

**Scoring and winning**
9. A hit shows only a neutral marker, so nobody can tell which ship it was. A ship's full picture appears only when every square of it has been found.
10. The scoreboard tracks shots left, hits with accuracy, ships still afloat and the level.
11. If the class sinks every ship, the class wins. If the shots run out first, the teacher survives and the hidden ships are shown.

### Modes, settings and options
- **Classic Fleet (Grades 7–12):** Navy ships on a sea grid. This is the default style.
- **Five K–6 picture styles:** Sea Safari, Dino Dig, Space Explorer, Spring Egg Hunt and Jungle Safari. The rules stay the same. Only the pictures, wording and sounds change.
- **Easy, Normal and Hard:** More ships make Easy friendlier. Hard uses a bigger grid, so every shot counts.
- **Question banks:** Use the samples (General review and Grammar review) or your own banks, and tick exactly which questions to use.
- **Hide questions:** Hide the question card and read your own questions aloud, which is handy for quick oral review.
- **Sound on or off:** Short sound effects for misses, hits and sunk ships. Turn them off from setup or the teacher bar.
- **Display options:** Light, dark and high-contrast themes, bigger text and larger buttons for the projector.

### Tips for teachers
- Let a different student call the square each time, so the whole room is involved even when one student answers.
- Use 🎤 Pick a student for fair turns. Everyone gets a go before anyone repeats.
- Differentiate with difficulty: Easy for a quick win at the end of a lesson, Hard for a longer test-prep session.
- Press Show fleet at the end and talk about which squares would have been better guesses.

### Ideas by subject
- **Math:** Multiplication facts, equivalent fractions, order of operations, unit conversions.
- **ELA:** Grammar terms, vocabulary definitions, figurative language, story elements.
- **Science:** States of matter, body systems, food chains, lab safety.
- **Social studies:** State capitals, map skills, branches of government, timeline events.

### FAQ
**Q: Can students fire more than one shot per question?**  
A: No. The grid is locked while a question is up. ✓ Correct unlocks it for exactly one square, even if students click several squares quickly, and ✗ Incorrect gives no shot. An unused shot never carries over to the next question.

**Q: Is it suitable for kindergarten and lower elementary?**  
A: Yes. Five picture styles (Sea Safari, Dino Dig, Space Explorer, Spring Egg Hunt and Jungle Safari) are made for Grades K–6. The questions are up to you, so you can keep them as simple as you need.

**Q: Can I use it without a question bank?**  
A: Yes. Press Hide questions and ask your own questions out loud. Press Next question for each one, then ✓ Correct or ✗ Incorrect as usual. A correct answer still earns exactly one shot.

**Q: How long does a game take?**  
A: It depends on the difficulty and how quickly you move through questions. Normal has 21 shots, so a game usually fits into one review session.

**Tag:** Quiz board

### Intro
Brain Board Blitz is a classic five-category quiz board for the front of the room. Teams take turns choosing a category and a point value, a student answers against an optional timer, and the scores update live.

Use it for end-of-unit and test review in any subject from Grades 3 to 12. Play one round or up to five. With the full version, you can save the game and finish it next lesson.

### At a glance
- Grades: Grades 3–12
- Subjects: Any subject · Unit and test review
- Group size: 2 or more teams
- Time: 20–45 minutes

### How to play Brain Board Blitz

**Set up the game**
1. Choose a question set and a class roster. The board needs five complete categories, each with questions at levels 1 to 5.
2. Pick the number of rounds (1–5) and the Round 1 point values (1–5, 10–50, 100–500 or 200–1000). Each later round adds a step: Round 2 is double Round 1, Round 3 is triple, and so on.
3. Set the skips per team (unlimited or 1–5), then press Start game. You need at least two active teams.

**Play a round**
4. The team in control picks a category and a value. The question opens in a large window.
5. Press 🎲 Pick to choose a student from that team. The picker is fair, so everyone gets a turn before anyone repeats.
6. Start the timer if you want one (10, 20, 30, 45 or 60 seconds), then press 👁 Reveal answer.
7. Mark the answer ✓ Correct, ✕ Incorrect, or Skip / no points. Used squares are marked on the board.
8. When every square is used, press ⏭ Next round. Later rounds use questions you haven't played yet.

**Scoring and winning**
9. A correct answer adds the points, and the team keeps control.
10. An incorrect answer takes the points away, and control passes to the next team.
11. Skip / no points uses one of that team's skips. You can still fix any score with the − / + buttons, and ↶ Undo reverses the last answer.
12. The team with the most points after the last round wins.

### Modes, settings and options
- **1 to 5 rounds:** A quick one-round game or a longer tournament. Point values grow each round.
- **Skip limits:** Unlimited skips, or 1–5 per team for the whole game, so teams can't skip every hard question.
- **Save and resume:** *(Full version badge)* The game saves itself after every answer. Pick it up next lesson with 📂 Resume a saved game.
- **Point values:** 1–5, 10–50, 100–500 or 200–1000 for Round 1.
- **Timer:** Optional: 10, 20, 30, 45 or 60 seconds, with a sound at zero.
- **Question library:** Load, save and delete sets, add questions by category and level, import or export CSV, or paste rows from Google Sheets. A readiness check shows any missing category or level.
- **Team rosters:** Paste one student per line as Team, Student, and untick absent students after loading.

### Tips for teachers
- Write level 1 questions everyone can answer, and save level 5 for stretch questions. That gives every student a chance to score.
- Set skips to 1 or 2 per team so teams try the harder squares instead of passing.
- Use the timer for older classes, and turn it off for younger students or English learners.
- With the full version, play Round 1 one day and resume the saved game the next for a two-day review.

### Ideas by subject
- **ELA:** Categories for vocabulary, grammar, literary devices, authors and genres.
- **Math:** Categories for fractions, geometry, word problems, measurement and mental math.
- **Science:** Categories for cells, ecosystems, forces, the water cycle and lab safety.
- **Social studies:** Categories for U.S. geography, civics, key people, events and vocabulary.

**Free version, extra line:** Save and resume, for finishing a game next lesson, is in the full version.

**Full version, extra line:** Save and resume is full version only: the game saves itself after every answer, and saved games are kept in the browser and in the Games tab of your own question spreadsheet.

### FAQ
**Q: How many questions do I need?**  
A: One question for each of the 5 categories at each of the 5 levels (25 in all) for one round. For more rounds, add more per category and level. The readiness check shows exactly what's missing.

**Q: Can I stop and finish the game next lesson?**  
A: Yes, in the full version. The game saves itself after every answer, and 💾 Save saves it whenever you like. Next lesson, choose 📂 Resume a saved game. Save and resume isn't included in the free version.

**Q: Can I correct a mistake?**  
A: Yes. ↶ Undo reverses the last answer, and every team score has − / + buttons.

**Q: How many teams can play?**  
A: At least two. Teams come from your class roster, and you can untick absent students.

**Tag:** Quiz show

### Intro
Million Point Climb is a multiple-choice quiz show for the front of the room, like the TV quiz shows students know. One contestant at a time climbs a points ladder, with lifelines, safety nets and the option to walk away.

Students answer out loud, so no student devices are needed. It suits Grades 4–12 in any subject, and the questions can get harder as the ladder climbs.

### At a glance
- Grades: Grades 4–12
- Subjects: Any subject
- Group size: One contestant at a time, with the whole class following along
- Time: 5–10 minutes per contestant

### How to play Million Point Climb

**Set up the game**
1. Choose how to run it. Basic uses one screen: the question fills the screen, and a slim teacher bar sits along the bottom. Presenter (full version) gives you a clean Display for the TV and a private Controller on your laptop (or phone) that shows you the answer.
2. Highlight one or more question sets and add them to the session. Each question has four choices, and can also have a difficulty, a category, a hint and an explanation.
3. Check the class session settings: the prize ladder, the prize prefix, the safety-net questions and the lifelines. Add your students, one name per line.
4. Press Start new class session.

**Play a round**
5. Choose the next contestant. Each student plays one climb per class session.
6. Read the question. The contestant picks an answer, and you select it.
7. Press the main button to step through Lock in, then Reveal, then Next question. It's one button, so you can't press the wrong one.
8. The contestant can use each lifeline once: 50:50, Ask the Audience, Phone a Friend, Switch the question and Ask the Host.
9. The contestant can walk away with their points, or keep climbing.

**Scoring and winning**
10. Each correct answer moves the contestant up the ladder. The ladder shows the current points and the guaranteed amount.
11. Safety-net questions (questions 5 and 10 by default) lock in points, so a wrong answer later drops the contestant back to the last safety net.
12. A results table records each contestant's name, points and outcome for the session.

### Modes, settings and options

- **Presenter: Display + Controller:** *(Full version badge)* Open the Display on the TV or an extended screen. Your Controller is the only screen that shows the correct answer.
- **Phone remote:** *(Full version badge)* In Presenter mode, open the same game link on your phone, signed in to the same Google account, and enter the 4-digit code from the Display. Expect a 1–3 second delay per tap, and keep the laptop window open.
- **Fastest Finger First:** An optional ordering round to choose the next contestant. Students write the order on paper, and the game shows the correct order.
- **Prize ladder:** Your own list of 5–30 rising values, with a prize prefix ($ by default).
- **Safety nets:** Choose which question numbers lock in points.
- **Lifelines:** Ask the Audience is simulated on screen. Phone a Friend can be a real classmate or a simulated friend, with 30 seconds. Ask the Host uses each question's hint and can be turned off.
- **Increasing difficulty:** Questions get harder as the ladder climbs, using the difficulty levels in your question sets.

### Tips for teachers
- Have the class answer on mini whiteboards while the contestant thinks, so everyone works on every question.
- Use the real-classmate option for Phone a Friend, so a second student gets to explain their thinking.
- Add a short explanation to each question and read it out after Reveal to turn mistakes into teaching moments.
- Play a short Fastest Finger First round to pick contestants fairly.

- **ELA:** Vocabulary in context, grammar choices, literary terms, author's purpose.

## Forbidden Words

**URL:** https://reviewrecess.com/games/forbidden-words/  
**Title tag:** Forbidden Words: Vocabulary Guessing Game | Review Recess  
**Meta description (154 characters):** Students explain a key term without saying the forbidden words while the class guesses. Team and pair modes, a timer and fair turns for vocabulary review.  
**H1:** Forbidden Words: a vocabulary game where students explain without the forbidden words  
**Tag:** Vocabulary speaking game

### Intro
Forbidden Words is a speaking game for vocabulary review. A student explains the word on the card without saying any of the forbidden words underneath it, while the class tries to guess it before the timer runs out.

Because students have to explain ideas in their own words, it's great for academic vocabulary in any subject. It suits Grades 5–12 and works with 2–10 teams or 2–20 pairs.

### At a glance
- Grades: Grades 5–12
- Subjects: Vocabulary in any subject
- Group size: 2–10 teams, or 2–20 pairs
- Time: 15–40 minutes

### How to play Forbidden Words

**Set up the game**
1. Choose Team mode or Pair mode.
2. Choose a card deck. Each card is a word plus its forbidden words.
3. Set the round length (30, 45, 60, 90 or 120 seconds), what happens when someone calls "Forbidden!", and who guesses.
4. Enter your teams and students (or load a roster), or set up your pairs, then press ▶ Start competition.

**Play a round**
5. Choose which team goes next and tap a student to come up and explain. The next student in rotation is suggested.
6. Press Start. The student explains the word on the card without saying it or any of the "Don't say" words.
7. Press ✔ Correct when someone guesses it, ⏭ Pass to skip the card, or 🚫 Forbidden! if a forbidden word slips out.
8. When time runs out, the Time's up screen shows how many cards were correct, passed and forbidden. Press Next for the next team.

**Scoring and winning**
9. Each correct guess is +1 for the explainer's team (or pair).
10. "Forbidden!" follows your setting: skip the card with no penalty (the default), −1 for the explaining side, or +1 for the team that called it.
11. ↶ Undo fixes a mis-tap. 🏆 Scoreboard shows the team scoreboard or the pair leaderboard at any time.

### Modes, settings and options
- **Team mode:** Call students up one at a time. The whole class guesses, and the points go to the student's team.
- **Pair mode:** Partners come to the screen in turn. One explains and the other guesses, and they swap next time. The class watches for forbidden words.
- **Who guesses:** The whole class (points still go to the explainer's team) or only the explainer's team.
- **Round length:** 30, 45, 60, 90 or 120 seconds.
- **Forbidden penalty:** No penalty, −1 for the explaining side, or +1 for the team that called it.
- **Fixed-size card:** The word, the forbidden words, the timer and the buttons stay in the same place whether a card has one line or two.
- **Saved competition:** The competition is saved in the browser, so you can resume it next lesson.

### Tips for teachers
- Model one card yourself first, so students hear what a good explanation sounds like.
- For younger students or English learners, use fewer forbidden words and a longer round.
- Make one student per team the forbidden-word judge to keep the whole class listening.
- Afterward, ask students to write a definition for any card that was passed.

### Ideas by subject
- **Science:** Photosynthesis, erosion, gravity or ecosystem, with forbidden words like plant, sun or water.
- **Social studies:** Democracy, treaty, migration, amendment.
- **ELA:** Literary terms such as metaphor, protagonist and theme.
- **Math:** Perimeter, denominator, parallel, variable.

### FAQ
**Q: Does a team lose a point for saying a forbidden word?**  
A: Only if you choose that. The default skips the card with no penalty. You can also choose −1 for the explaining side, or +1 for the team that called it.

**Q: Can students play in pairs instead of teams?**  
A: Yes. Pair mode takes 2–20 pairs. Partners take turns explaining and guessing, with a pair leaderboard.

**Q: How do I choose who comes up?**  
A: Before each turn, tap a student's name. The next student in rotation is suggested, so everyone gets a turn.

**Q: Are there keyboard shortcuts?**  
A: Yes: C for correct, P for pass, F for forbidden, Space to pause and Ctrl/⌘+Z to undo.

## Letter Lifeline

**Title tag:** Letter Lifeline: Spelling Word Guessing Game | Review Recess  
**Meta description (154 characters):** A picture-based alternative to hangman for spelling and vocabulary. Guess the letters before the picture runs out, with clues, 20 themes and team scoring.  
**H1:** Letter Lifeline: a picture-based alternative to hangman for spelling and vocabulary  
**Tag:** Spelling & vocabulary

### Intro
Letter Lifeline is a guess-the-letters word game with friendly pictures instead of a gallows. Every wrong letter takes a visible piece away from the picture, and the class has to find the word before it's gone.

Use it for spelling lists or unit vocabulary from Grades 1–12. An optional clue line under each word turns guessing into recall practice. Play 1 vs 1, in teams or as a whole class.

### At a glance
- Grades: Grades 1–12 (Classic pictures for K–6, Skeleton for 7–12)
- Subjects: Spelling and vocabulary in any subject
- Group size: 1 vs 1, 2–4 teams (up to 6 from a roster), or the whole class
- Time: 10–30 minutes

### How to play Letter Lifeline

**Set up the game**
1. Choose the game mode: 1 vs 1, Teams (2, 3 or 4, or up to 6 from a roster) or Whole Class.
2. Choose the game length: Quick (6), Standard (10), Epic (15) or Mega (20) wrong guesses.
3. Choose a picture style: Classic (K–6), with 20 themes or 🎲 Surprise me, or Skeleton (7–12), a classroom anatomy model.
4. Set the options (💡 Hints and 🏅 Auto-award points, each on or off), choose your word bank and press START GAME.

**Play a round**
5. The word appears as blanks. Spaces, hyphens and symbols are shown, and only the letters are hidden.
6. The player or team guesses a letter, and you click it on the on-screen A–Z buttons. Correct letters turn green and wrong ones turn red.
7. A correct letter keeps the turn. A wrong letter removes a piece of the picture and passes the turn to the next player or team.
8. If a team thinks it knows the word, press 💡 Guess the answer and mark it ✓ Correct or ✗ Incorrect.
9. Press Next word ➜ when the word is solved or the picture runs out.

**Scoring and winning**
10. With Auto-award on, whoever guesses the last letter gets +1, and the message names the scorer.
11. Guess the answer: a correct guess is +2 and solves the word, and an incorrect guess is −1 and passes the turn.
12. 👁 Reveal shows the answer with no points. Every scoreboard card has −1 / +1 buttons, and ↶ Undo steps back one letter.
13. Press 🏁 End game for the final scores.

### Modes, settings and options
- **1 vs 1, Teams or Whole Class:** Head-to-head, 2–4 teams (up to 6 from a roster), or the whole class against the picture.
- **Classic pictures (K–6):** 20 themes, such as a pizza, a snowman and a rocket, or 🎲 Surprise me for a new theme every word.
- **Skeleton (Grades 7–12):** A classroom anatomy model loses a bone or part with each wrong guess and topples over when the guesses run out.
- **Game length:** 6, 10, 15 or 20 wrong guesses. Every wrong letter visibly changes the picture at every length.
- **Hints:** On shows the word bank's clue under the word. Off hides it for a harder game.
- **Auto-award points:** On gives +1 to whoever guesses the last letter. Off leaves scoring to you.
- **Word banks:** Words with optional clues, such as the River Valley Civilizations sample.
- **Reduced motion:** The pictures follow the light, dark and high-contrast themes and respect your computer's reduce-motion setting.

### Tips for teachers
- Write each clue as a definition, so students practice meaning as well as spelling.
- Turn Hints off for a second play-through of the same list.
- Use Whole Class mode for younger students, and Teams with Auto-award for competitive older classes.
- Pick Quick (6) for a challenge, or Mega (20) to give younger classes more chances.

### Ideas by subject
- **ELA:** Weekly spelling words, vocabulary from a novel, prefixes and suffixes.
- **Science:** Key terms with definitions as clues: habitat, condensation, magnet.
- **Social studies:** People, places and terms from the unit, such as Mesopotamia or pharaoh.
- **Math:** Math vocabulary: perimeter, quotient, parallelogram.

### FAQ
**Q: Is this a hangman game?**  
A: It plays like the classic letter-guessing game, but with friendly pictures instead of a gallows. Each wrong letter takes a piece away from a pizza, a snowman, a rocket and more, or from a skeleton model for older students.

**Q: Can I hide the clue?**  
A: Yes. Turn Hints off on the setup screen to hide the clue line in every mode.

**Q: Does every wrong guess change the picture?**

### Intro
The Tic-Tac-Toe Quiz Game puts a review question behind every square. Two teams, X and O, take turns choosing a square and answering. A correct answer claims it, and three in a row wins the round.

### At a glance
- Grades: Grades 2–12
- Subjects: Any subject
- Group size: 2 teams: X vs O
- Time: 10–30 minutes

### How to play Tic-Tac-Toe Quiz Game

**Set up the game**
1. Type the names of Team X and Team O, and list each team's players, one per line. Players rotate in order.
2. Load a saved question bank, or type the questions as question | answer, one per line, and save the bank.
3. Choose the game mode: Standard (a wrong answer ends the turn) or Steal Mode (the other team gets one chance).
4. Press START GAME.

**Play a round**
5. The team whose turn it is picks a square, and a question appears.
6. The player whose turn it is answers. Press REVEAL ANSWER.
7. Press ✓ CORRECT to claim the square, or ✕ INCORRECT. In Steal Mode, the other team's next player then gets one chance at the same question.
8. Play passes to the other team. Keep going until a team gets three in a row or the board is full.

**Scoring and winning**
9. Three in a row wins the round, with confetti and a win sound. A full board with no winner is a draw.
10. Each team has a rounds-won counter. Press NEXT ROUND to play again.
11. A new round keeps the remaining questions and the player rotation.

### Modes, settings and options
- **Standard:** A wrong answer ends the turn.
- **Steal Mode:** After a wrong answer, the other team's next player gets one chance to steal the square.
- **Teacher panel:** ↶ Undo last move, ⏭ Skip question, ↺ New round, sound on or off, full screen and 🏁 End game.
- **Question banks:** Save as many banks as you like, load them next lesson, or open the question sheet to edit them all at once.
- **No repeats:** Questions are shuffled, and none repeats until every question has been used.

### Tips for teachers
- Use Steal Mode to keep the other team listening on every question.
- Let the team talk it over for 10 seconds, but only the player whose turn it is may answer.
- Play best of three or best of five rounds.
- Talk about strategy with older classes: the center square and corners matter.

- **Math:** Quick computation, fractions and word problems.

A: When a team answers incorrectly, the other team's next player gets one chance at the same question.

**Q: Can I fix a mistake?**  
A: Yes. ↶ Undo last move is in the teacher panel.

**Q: Do questions carry over between rounds?**  
A: Yes. A new round keeps the remaining questions and the player rotation.
