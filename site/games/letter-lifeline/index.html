Letter Lifeline: a picture-based alternative to hangman for spelling and vocabulary

Letter Lifeline is a guess-the-letters word game with friendly pictures instead of a gallows. Every wrong letter takes a visible piece away from the picture, and the class has to find the word before it's gone.

Use it for spelling lists or unit vocabulary from Grades 1–12. An optional clue line under each word turns guessing into recall practice. Play 1 vs 1, in teams or as a whole class.

Step by step

How to play Letter Lifeline

Set up the game

  1. Choose the game mode: 1 vs 1, Teams (2, 3 or 4, or up to 6 from a roster) or Whole Class.
  2. Choose the game length: Quick (6), Standard (10), Epic (15) or Mega (20) wrong guesses.
  3. Choose a picture style: Classic (K–6), with 20 themes or 🎲 Surprise me, or Skeleton (7–12), a classroom anatomy model.
  4. Set the options (💡 Hints and 🏅 Auto-award points, each on or off), choose your word bank and press START GAME.

Play a round

  1. The word appears as blanks. Spaces, hyphens and symbols are shown, and only the letters are hidden.
  2. The player or team guesses a letter, and you click it on the on-screen A–Z buttons. Correct letters turn green and wrong ones turn red.
  3. A correct letter keeps the turn. A wrong letter removes a piece of the picture and passes the turn to the next player or team.
  4. If a team thinks it knows the word, press 💡 Guess the answer and mark it ✓ Correct or ✗ Incorrect.
  5. Press Next word ➜ when the word is solved or the picture runs out.

Scoring and winning

  1. With Auto-award on, whoever guesses the last letter gets +1, and the message names the scorer.
  2. Guess the answer: a correct guess is +2 and solves the word, and an incorrect guess is −1 and passes the turn.
  3. 👁 Reveal shows the answer with no points. Every scoreboard card has −1 / +1 buttons, and ↶ Undo steps back one letter.
  4. Press 🏁 End game for the final scores.

Make it fit your class

Letter Lifeline modes, settings and options

1 vs 1, Teams or Whole Class

Head-to-head, 2–4 teams (up to 6 from a roster), or the whole class against the picture.

Classic pictures (K–6)

20 themes, such as a pizza, a snowman and a rocket, or 🎲 Surprise me for a new theme every word.

Skeleton (Grades 7–12)

A classroom anatomy model loses a bone or part with each wrong guess and topples over when the guesses run out.

Game length

6, 10, 15 or 20 wrong guesses. Every wrong letter visibly changes the picture at every length.

Hints

On shows the word bank's clue under the word. Off hides it for a harder game.

Auto-award points

On gives +1 to whoever guesses the last letter. Off leaves scoring to you.

Word banks

Words with optional clues, such as the River Valley Civilizations sample.

Reduced motion

The pictures follow the light, dark and high-contrast themes and respect your computer's reduce-motion setting.

From the classroom

Tips for teachers

  • Write each clue as a definition, so students practice meaning as well as spelling.
  • Turn Hints off for a second play-through of the same list.
  • Use Whole Class mode for younger students, and Teams with Auto-award for competitive older classes.
  • Pick Quick (6) for a challenge, or Mega (20) to give younger classes more chances.

Ideas by subject

Ways to use it across the curriculum

ELA

Weekly spelling words, vocabulary from a novel, prefixes and suffixes.

Science

Key terms with definitions as clues: habitat, condensation, magnet.

Social studies

People, places and terms from the unit, such as Mesopotamia or pharaoh.

Math

Math vocabulary: perimeter, quotient, parallelogram.

Questions

Letter Lifeline FAQ

Is this a hangman game?

It plays like the classic letter-guessing game, but with friendly pictures instead of a gallows. Each wrong letter takes a piece away from a pizza, a snowman, a rocket and more, or from a skeleton model for older students.

Can I hide the clue?

Yes. Turn Hints off on the setup screen to hide the clue line in every mode.

Does every wrong guess change the picture?

Yes, at every game length. Each picture is split to match the number of wrong guesses you choose.

Does it store student scores?

No. Letter Lifeline doesn't save scores or student data.
